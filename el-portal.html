Launch, scale & ownership

A working core.
Then deliberate expansion.

January 2024 launch

The new case-study narrative dates the core build to April 2023–January 2024, approximately nine months, with a four-person core team.

Reported scale

The author-provided narrative reports 5,000+ students on the platform. The estimated 75–80% share of the student experience is an author estimate, not an analytics measurement.

Research changed the design

Direct focus groups, roundtables, surveys, and learner interviews exposed gaps in the inherited process. Discovery shifted from reproducing legacy screens to understanding the work.

Sequenced delivery

The core registration experience shipped first. Clinical-site onboarding and practicum workflows expanded later; not every module was present on launch day.

Source: “Elportal numbers” author narrative. These are reported delivery facts and estimates, not independently audited impact metrics. The earlier upgrade business case contains projected costs and savings; those projections are not realized outcomes.

Launch outcomes & lessons

Staff capacity freed.
A clearer student experience.

30+ staff reassigned

After EL Portal launched, Nightingale was able to reassign more than 30 internal staff members to other assignments within the organization.

Less friction for students

Students could register faster and find class information more easily. Communication between students and internal teams noticeably improved.

Real use exposed training needs

The successful launch also revealed that internal teams needed more training. Customer service calls surfaced difficulties with workflows, including the swap feature that lets students exchange class times. The team identified workflow adjustments in response.

Monitoring shaped the next release

We continued monitoring the application after launch and held new meetings with stakeholders. Those discussions shaped the scope of the next release, which we then delivered.

Outcomes reported by Charious McLaurin. The 30+ figure describes staff reassignment within the organization. Registration, information access, and communication improvements are qualitative observations; measured time savings and financial savings have not been supplied.

    Workflow architecture

    Different experiences.
    Shared coordination rules.

    VCBC

    Administrators create session capacity, faculty claim and schedule slots, and eligible learners enroll. The walkthrough describes a maximum of 10 learners per slot.

    Design concern: availability, eligibility, rescheduling boundaries, and explicit admin exception handling.

    DFC rotations

    Partner-site dates and learner capacity shape group assignments. Scheduler and credentialing responsibilities can be separated through configurable permissions.

    Design concern: prevent over-assignment and surface unfinished site-specific requirements.

    Practicum

    Individual placements have site requirements, acceptance decisions, and coordination needs. Graduate learners may propose a site for review.

    Design concern: make site approval and learner readiness visible before participation.

    Tasks & 360 profiles

    Connect assignments, deadlines, compliance, schedules, and notes in a role-aware view. Separate staff-only coordination from learner-visible actions.

    Design concern: information visibility and permission to act are separate decisions.

    Post-launch learning

    Real operational friction
    reveals the architecture.

    Cross-system identity

    Issue discussions surfaced identity updates that did not appear consistently across operational tools.

    Architecture lesson: define source ownership, synchronization expectations, and a way to diagnose stale data.

    Sorting that matches work

    Stakeholder review exposed a date-ordering behavior that did not match the intended sequence.

    Architecture lesson: reproduce real workflows and test sort semantics against meaningful operational examples.

    Role combinations

    An access request required distinguishing operational oversight from restricted academic information.

    Architecture lesson: model permission scope explicitly rather than relying only on broad role names.

    Visible delivery status

    Stakeholders needed a dependable way to see the status of submitted issues.

    My contribution in the discussion: offer read-only visibility into the delivery board and clarify the feedback path.

    My architectural perspective

    Automate the coordination.
    Keep policy deterministic.

    EL Portal demonstrates the foundation behind my AI approach: explicit roles, source boundaries, state transitions, and exceptions. Required eligibility and scheduling rules belong in testable application behavior. AI can assist with explanation and coordination only within an approved scope.

    Rules, AI & safe actions

    Use the existing Portal.
    Add help where it earns trust.

    LayerArchitecture approachWhy it matters
    Priority & evidenceUse deterministic ranking and structured Portal records. Route stale or conflicting credential evidence to reconciliation.Staff can reproduce the order and see why a case needs attention. AI does not clear a credential.
    AI generationUse one institution-approved model through a server-side gateway, with limited inputs and validated output formats. Fall back to approved templates.Centralize quality, cost, latency, model version, and permitted data handling.
    Approval & executionStore proposals with evidence, freshness, version, and expiry where relevant. Bind approval to the exact content and recheck live rules before execution.A changed proposal or stale source must not inherit an earlier approval.
    Scheduling & notificationsUse transactional seat allocation and duplicate-safe event handling. Retry notifications after a committed change without sending duplicates.Concurrent requests cannot claim the same seat. A failed schedule change preserves the prior slot.
    Identity & auditKeep existing sign-in and permission bundles. Add server-side checks for permitted rows and fields, and record reviewer and outcome.Only authorized people can view evidence or approve consequential actions.

    The Portal can refresh priorities, prepare drafts and briefings, route data exceptions, and continue configured reminders. A person approves each proposed discretionary message, credential decision, and schedule or placement decision.

    AI architecture · Decisions and safeguards

    Help staff act sooner.
    Keep placement rules explicit.

    The four AI-assisted workflows are confirmed shipped. The architecture separates readiness ranking, AI explanations and drafts, and the existing Portal rules that control scheduling and placement.

    Rank from structured evidence

    Use rotation deadlines, open tasks, and credential freshness to prioritize review. Show the reason and source so staff can inspect why a learner needs attention.

    Draft within a limited scope

    The implementation plan defines a server-side model gateway, limited inputs, validated output formats, and approved-template fallback. AI prepares help; authorized staff make credential and placement decisions.

    Recheck before committing

    Bind approval to the exact proposal. Recheck current eligibility, capacity, and cutoff rules before executing a change. Keep seat allocation and notification retries safe from duplicate actions.

    Success measures & operating controls

    Measure useful help.
    Protect the placement decision.

    OutcomeProposed measureInterpretation
    Faster resolutionMedian time from an actionable blocker to coordinator disposition at least 20% below baseline; the 90th percentile no worse than baseline.Compare similar rotation windows and cohorts. Report case counts, exclusions, and missing data.
    Earlier actionable flagsMeasure missed deadlines flagged at least two business days early and the share of reviewed flags that led to meaningful action.Compare at equal reviews per shift. Use the rules-based queue as the baseline for any future predictor.
    Better readinessUnresolved required items at cutoff per 100 scheduled learners, grouped by site and rotation type.Measure readiness without relaxing eligibility or credential rules.
    Less drafting effortDraft-to-approval time, major edits or rejections per 100 drafts, duplicate sends, and unsupported claims.If the queue helps but drafts need frequent correction, keep the queue and improve drafting separately.
    Safe, traceable changesZero unauthorized commits, stale-evidence clearances, capacity breaches, or duplicate sends in integrated release tests; target complete audit records for consequential commits.These are release-test and traceability targets, not observed production results.
    Operating controls defined in the implementation plan
    • Education, Credentialing, and Scheduling agree on exception authority, deadlines, cutoff rules, and permitted overrides.
    • Source owners provide stable IDs, freshness timestamps, test access, and a way to reconcile conflicting records.
    • Portal engineering validates scheduling, placement, messaging, and audit commands, including safe retries and concurrent requests.
    • Privacy, security, and procurement approve permitted model inputs, processing destinations, retention, and provider terms.
    • Operations supplies approved templates, review capacity, a test inbox, and named support and incident owners.
    • Engineering and operations provide staging, feature flags, monitoring, and a rehearsed rollback.

    Credential freshness, clear exception authority, and review capacity remain important operating controls. The measures above are targets from the implementation plan; measured before-and-after outcomes have not yet been supplied.
